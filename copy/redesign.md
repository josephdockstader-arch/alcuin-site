## graduate-intro
Fluency is the easy half of the outcome. The graduate we describe can also doubt the tool — challenge what it returns, check it, and decide when the work should stay theirs. A school produces that graduate in a sequence, and the sequence is the reason the work is ordered the way it is.

## grad-1-title
A base without the tool

## grad-1-body
Judgment depends on knowledge. A student who knows history can notice a misplaced event in a summary, and a student who can calculate can recognize an implausible total. Graduates need a substantial base of knowledge and skills they can use without assistance.

## grad-2-title
Fluency on real work

## grad-2-body
In practical work, the graduate chooses a suitable model, supplies the information it needs, and reviews its output. They may use AI to gather information, draft, test, organize, or translate.

## grad-3-title
Skepticism and judgment

## grad-3-body
The graduate checks important claims against independent sources, notices when a chatbot agrees too readily, and can judge when to use the tool, when not to, and what using it implies.

## engagement-intro
We recommend opening with an on-site, whole-staff in-service. It starts as a diagnostic — where AI is already affecting what your staff does well, examined on your school's own materials — and the support that follows runs through the year.

## eng-2-title
