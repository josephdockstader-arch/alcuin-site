## purpose-heading
From broad principles to classroom expectations

## purpose-body
Eight strands organize 37 standards across four stages, from kindergarten through graduation. Each standard describes something students can demonstrate, giving teachers a basis for planning lessons and discussing progress.

Use the strands to identify where AI literacy belongs in your curriculum. Use the grade-band expectations to decide what to teach and what students should be able to do independently.

## strands-heading
Eight areas of learning

## strands-intro
Each strand connects knowledge about AI with decisions students need to make.

## strands
- **Foundations** — The knowledge and skills students need to be able to use without AI.
- **Thinking With AI** — How AI use affects learning, effort, and judgment.
- **Truth and Evidence** — Checking claims and recognizing unreliable or misleading output.
- **The Machine** — How AI systems work: how they are trained, what they produce, and where they fall short.
- **Working With AI** — Choosing tools, supplying context, prompting, and reviewing results.
- **Data and Ethics** — Privacy, bias, consent, intellectual property, and responsibility.
- **AI and the World** — AI's influence on work, information, power, and public life.
- **Shaping AI** — Evaluating AI systems and deciding how they should be improved or used.

## sample-eyebrow
A standard in practice

## sample-heading
Verify claims before relying on them

## sample-meta
TE.2 · Truth and Evidence · Core

## sample-statement
Check AI-generated claims against independent sources and justify accepting, revising, or rejecting them.

## sample-intro
The code names the strand and the standard: TE.2 is the second standard in Truth and Evidence. Core standards are expected of every student. The expectations develop across four stages, from the early grades to graduation.

## sample-stage-1
**K–2 · Emerging** — Suggests one way the class could check a claim in an AI answer.

## sample-stage-2
**3–5 · Guided** — Compares an AI answer with a provided source and explains which account to trust.

## sample-stage-3
**6–8 · Independent** — Checks a claim against two sources, including one located independently, and records the result.

## sample-stage-4
**9–12 · Anticipating** — Includes a verification plan in the project proposal, carries it out, documents results, and resolves discrepancies.

## norms-heading
Classroom norms for AI use

## norms-intro
These twelve norms give students a shared set of expectations. Teachers can use them to discuss choices students make while working with AI.

## norms
1. Review the first answer.
2. Make the work your own.
3. Keep thinking throughout the task.
4. Take responsibility for work with your name on it.
5. Check confident answers.
6. Consider another point of view.
7. Keep a record of AI use.
8. Follow the rules for the assignment.
9. Learn how to use the available tools.
10. Check what you learned.
11. Protect personal information.
12. Know when to work without AI.
